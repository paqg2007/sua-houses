Walking La Candelaria

The places we love around our homes, and where we like to eat. Everything here is on foot, or a short ride away.

Tourist places Where to eat

TOURIST PLACES

Walk it, slowly

Museums, squares, churches and markets, from our doorstep to a day trip out of town.

Plazoleta del Chorro de Quevedo

Have a walk, listen to cuenteros, have some chicha and dinner in the local restaurants. El Chorro is surrounded by myths about the foundation of the city. This square used to be the border between the Spanish city, Pueblo Viejo, and the indigenous one, Thibsaquillo. El Chorro is one of the most iconic landmarks in Bogotá.

Santuario Nuestra Señora del Carmen

Gothic and Byzantine styles frame this catholic temple.

Military Museum

More than 200 years of Colombian military history.

Museo Botero

This museum is home to the private art collection of our most famous artist, Fernando Botero, and several of his own works.

Free entrance

Luis Ángel Arango Library

The most important public library in Bogotá. It hosts different cultural activities such as concerts, expositions, workshops, and more.

Teatro Colón

Appreciate neoclassic architecture in the most elegant theatre in Bogotá.

Check the program

Museo Colonial

Find collections of paintings, sculptures, textiles, silver, and furniture from colonial times.

La Concordia Market

Renovated space from what used to be a real plaza de mercado. La Concordia is a good place to try chicha, guarapo, the Muisca alcoholic drinks, ajiaco, the potato soup from Bogotá, and lechona.

Plaza de Bolívar

The most important and emblematic landmark in Bogotá, Colombia. In our main square you will find some of the most important government buildings in town: Palacio de Justicia, Catedral Primada de Colombia, Capitolio Nacional, Palacio de Liévano, the Mayor's office, and Colegio de San Bartolomé. Feed the birds, pet llamas, and take pictures.

Museo de la Independencia Casa del Florero

Visit the house where the Llorente vase incident, which sparked the protests that led to the Independence of Colombia on July 20, 1810, took place: the Museum of Independence, Flower Vase House, located in Bolívar Square. Here you can see about 2,000 objects related to the history of the country's independence.

Casa de Nariño

House of the presidents of Colombia. Find it at Bolívar Square.

Book a tour

Museo Claustro de San Agustín

Now part of the National University of Colombia, this venue has museological collections.

Free entrance · Carrera 8 No. 7-21

Pasaje Rivas

The first Colombian mall was built in 1893. Here you can purchase handicrafts and baskets.

Museo del Oro

The museum you simply can't miss. Home to the most important archeological collections in Bogotá and the famous legend of El Dorado.

Free admission on Sundays

Quinta de Bolívar

Simón Bolívar's country house at the foot of Monserrate, now a museum with gardens and views over the city.

Monserrate

Bogotá's mountain sanctuary, 3,152 meters above the city. Go up by cable car or funicular for the best view of the whole city.

Mercado de las Pulgas San Alejo

Bogotá's famous Sunday flea market, with antiques, handicrafts and vintage finds.

Torre Colpatria

Bogotá's tallest tower, with a viewpoint on the 48th floor and a light show on its facade at night.

Plaza Cultural La Santamaría

The former bullring, now a cultural venue for concerts and events.

Museo Nacional

Colombia's oldest museum, in a former prison, with art, history and archaeology under one roof.

Cementerio Central

Bogotá's oldest cemetery, an outdoor museum of sculpture and history.

Paloquemao Market

The city's biggest market, a feast of fruit, flowers and local food.

Jardín Botánico de Bogotá

Nineteen hectares of gardens, orchids and native plants, with a tropical rainforest dome.

Catedral de Sal

An underground cathedral carved inside a salt mine in Zipaquirá, an hour from Bogotá.

Nemocón Salt Mine

A salt mine turned into a museum, with tunnels, mirrors of brine and a colonial town above.

Lake Guatavita

The sacred lake of the El Dorado legend, in the hills outside Bogotá.

Tranvía de Bogotá

Ride the red open tram through La Candelaria, with guides and characters from the city's history telling stories along the way. It boards in front of the Casa de Moneda and runs Wednesday to Sunday. Bogotá's first tram ran from 1884, pulled by mules and later electric, until 1951. You can still see its rails at Carrera 7 and Avenida Jiménez.

WHERE TO EAT

Come hungry

Our favorite tables in La Candelaria, from ajiaco to French pastry.

De Una Travel

A café restaurant on Calle 10, with food from the mountains and valleys of Colombia. Good for breakfast or a relaxed dinner.

Café de la Peña

Be amazed at Amparito's French pastry recipes. You can also admire Natalia Pontón's art.

Restaurante Fulanitos

Vallecaucana cooking in the heart of La Candelaria.

El Gato Gris

A classic of the Chorro de Quevedo square, with a terrace right on the plaza.

La Bruja

A former monastery turned restaurant, with a witch theme, live music and international dishes in a bohemian colonial house.

La Puerta Falsa

Bogotá's oldest sweet shop, famous for its tamales, hot chocolate and traditional sweets.

Crepes & Waffles, Las Aguas

Crepes, waffles and ice cream, a Colombian classic, steps from the university district.

Bogotá Beer Company

Craft beer brewed in Bogotá.

Casa Vieja

More than 40 years of tradition and one of the best ajiacos in Bogotá, in a colonial house on Avenida Jiménez. Save room for the postre de natas.

Casa Mamá Luz

Mamá Luz won the prize for the best ajiaco in Bogotá. Caribbean and traditional cooking in a small house on Calle 10. Go for lunch and go early, the line forms fast.

Hours and prices change often. Check the official site before you go. And if you want a tip while you are here, write to us, we love to talk about our neighborhood.
