TOURIST PLACES

Walk it, slowly

Museums, squares, churches and markets, from our doorstep to a day trip out of town.

Plazoleta del Chorro de Quevedo

Have a walk, listen to cuenteros, have some chicha and dinner in the local restaurants. El Chorro is surrounded by myths about the foundation of the city. This square used to be the border between the Spanish city, Pueblo Viejo, and the indigenous one, Thibsaquillo. El Chorro is one of the most iconic landmarks in Bogotá.

Tranvía de Bogotá

Ride the red open tram through La Candelaria, with guides and characters from the city's history telling stories along the way. It boards in front of the Casa de Moneda and runs Wednesday to Sunday. Bogotá's first tram ran from 1884, pulled by mules and later electric, until 1951. You can still see its rails at Carrera 7 and Avenida Jiménez.

HIDDEN GEMS

Two tables few tourists find

Our two favorite hidden restaurants in La Candelaria, a short walk from the house. Homemade food, old rooms, and all the time in the world.

Pimienta & Café

A small dining room in a colonial house on Carrera 3, run with energy and warmth by Miriam for more than 20 years. Walking in feels like entering someone's home, and the food tastes like it too. Lunch only, Monday to Saturday. On Sundays the door stays closed.

The ajiaco santafereño is what people come back for, creamy and generous, the way it should be. The chuleta valluna and the salmon in rosemary sauce are worth ordering too. Everything is homemade, portions are generous and prices are kind.

Cra 3 #9-27 · Lunch Mon to Sat, closed Sun · COP 20,000 to 40,000 · Walk · 288 m (0.18 mi) · about 4 min · Open in Google Maps →

Maria Candelaria

A casona more than 300 years old, run by its owners María and José, who cook every order from scratch in a wood oven. Antique furniture and old family photographs fill rooms that have barely changed in centuries. Service is slow on purpose, so come with time, and book ahead if you are more than three. Cash only.

Start with a taste of the soups before you choose. The tomato soup and the onion soup are both worth trying. Then the stuffed chicken breast, with mushrooms or olives, or the ajiaco. Every main dish comes with soup, natural juice and a basket of bread on the house. Save room for the cuajada with hot melao, or the chocolate volcano.

Cra 4 #8-58 · Lunch Wed to Sun, closed Mon and Tue · COP 20,000 to 40,000 · Cash only · Walk · 437 m (0.27 mi) · about 5 min · Open in Google Maps → · Call to reserve

ZONA M, LA MACARENA

A short walk north, a whole other table

Zona M is the gastronomic heart of La Macarena, a bohemian neighborhood about a 15 to 20 minute walk north of La Candelaria along Carrera 7, past the Museo Nacional. Its restaurant row runs along Carrera 4A between Calles 26 and 28, where small family-run kitchens, many of them open for decades, serve everything from handmade Italian pasta to Serbian goulash.

La Monferrina

A pasta factory and restaurant where the pasta is made fresh in house every day, and the carbonara is counted among the best in Bogotá. Chef Sebastián Bedoya has spent more than a decade perfecting fresh pasta.

Taxi · 2.2 km (1.39 mi) · about 5 min · around $8,700 COP (about $2.51) · Open in Google Maps → · Instagram →

Beograd

Bogotá's Serbian table, run for more than 20 years by Katarina Markovich, who arrived after the Yugoslav wars. Sarma, Karadjordje schnitzel, goulash and rakija, served in a small brick house on Calle 26B.

Taxi · 2.3 km (1.41 mi) · about 5 min · around $8,700 COP (about $2.51) · Open in Google Maps → · Instagram →

Panadería La Santamaría

A neighborhood bakery since 1974, and the classic spot for a Bogotá breakfast of tamal, hot chocolate and fresh bread.

Taxi · 2.3 km (1.41 mi) · about 5 min · around $8,700 COP (about $2.51) · Open in Google Maps →
